> **Internal use only.** This shows how a results-based abstract would read *after* the real fieldwork, using the simulated results in `results/fsqca_results.md`. The simulated firms encode our own assumptions, so these are not findings. Do not submit, circulate or cite.

**What did we do?** The EU's Digital Product Passport (DPP) will make product-level data on composition, origin, chemicals, footprint and recyclability a condition of selling garments in the EU. Most of these data sit with suppliers several tiers upstream. We asked which combinations of supplier conditions produce passport readiness, which produce its absence, and whether readiness leads to circularity.

**Why should readers care?** U.S. tariffs and the EU–India free trade agreement are steering Indian exporters toward the EU just as data become a condition of market access. The DPP is a supply chain design problem, not an IT purchase.

**Theoretical lens.** Organizational information processing theory, read configurationally, and institutional decoupling.

**Method and data.** fsQCA of [50] Indian textile and apparel exporters interviewed between December 2026 and February 2027.

**Findings (SIMULATED).** Two routes were sufficient for high readiness (solution consistency 0.92, coverage 0.77). In the integrated route, ownership of upstream stages and digital traceability were core. In the networked route, multi-tier data sharing and buyer pressure were core, with low appropriation concern or digital traceability as peripheral conditions. No condition was necessary: digital traceability fell just short (0.89), and certified firms reached readiness with modest systems. Buyer pressure was present among ready and unready firms alike; low readiness combined missing traceability with appropriation concern, *whatever* the pressure. Readiness alone was not sufficient for formal circular practices (consistency 0.52), while informal circularity was widespread regardless of data.

**Implications.** Suppliers build passport capacity by owning, negotiating or borrowing access to upstream data. Brands get better passports by limiting how they use supplier data than by pushing harder. Policy should recognise certification data, fund cluster data platforms, and make India's informal circular economy visible in the passport.

*Keywords:* digital product passport; circular economy; apparel supply chains; information processing theory; fsQCA; India
